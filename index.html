What Is USMLE? A Complete Guide to the United States Medical Licensing Examination

📅 Updated: January 2026 ⏱️ 12 min read 🩺 Medical Education

The United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE) is a three-step examination program that serves as the foundation for medical licensure in the United States. Sponsored by the Federation of State Medical Boards (FSMB) and the National Board of Medical Examiners (NBME), the USMLE assesses a physician's ability to apply medical knowledge, concepts, and fundamental patient-centered skills required for safe and effective patient care.

First implemented in 1992, the USMLE has been accepted by all 50 state medical boards as a common evaluation system for licensure applicants. For medical students and graduates—whether from U.S. or international medical schools—understanding the structure, purpose, and requirements of each Step is essential for planning a successful pathway to practicing medicine in the United States.

Key Takeaways

  • The USMLE is a 3-Step exam program required for U.S. medical licensure.
  • Step 1 is now pass/fail; Step 2 CK and Step 3 remain scored.
  • International medical graduates (IMGs) must obtain ECFMG Certification.
  • Registration for all Steps moved to the FSMB USMLE Portal in 2026.
  • You may take the same Step up to four times, with limits per year.

The Three-Step Structure

The USMLE consists of three separate examinations, each taken at different stages of medical education and training.

Step 1: Assessing Basic Sciences

Step 1 evaluates whether an examinee understands and can apply important concepts of the basic sciences fundamental to the practice of medicine. This includes disciplines such as anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, microbiology, pathology, and pharmacology. Typically taken after completing the pre-clerkship curriculum—usually between the second and third years of medical school—Step 1 emphasizes the principles and mechanisms underlying health, disease, and modes of therapy.

Since January 2022, Step 1 has been reported as pass/fail, with no numerical score released. The passing standard is set at a three-digit score of 196, and examinees generally need to answer roughly 60% of questions correctly to pass. A shift to pass/fail scoring was implemented to reduce the emphasis on high-stakes numerical ranking, though it has effectively transferred that pressure to Step 2 CK.

Step 2 CK: Evaluating Clinical Knowledge

Step 2 Clinical Knowledge (CK) assesses whether an examinee can apply medical knowledge and understanding of clinical science essential for providing patient care under supervision. This includes emphasis on health promotion, disease prevention, and principles of managed care. It is typically taken after completing core clinical rotations, often toward the end of the third year or the beginning of the fourth year of medical school.

Unlike Step 1, Step 2 CK remains a scored examination, and it now carries significant weight in residency applications. The passing score was raised to 218 in July 2025. For international medical graduates, a strong Step 2 CK score has become a critical differentiator in the residency match process.

Step 3: Testing Independent Clinical Decision-Making

Step 3 is the final examination in the USMLE sequence, typically taken during residency. It assesses whether an examinee can apply medical knowledge and understanding of biomedical and clinical science essential for the unsupervised practice of medicine, with emphasis on patient management in ambulatory settings. Because Step 3 covers core clinical disciplines, it is recommended to take it while this knowledge is still fresh, often during the first year of residency.

Who Takes the USMLE?

The USMLE is designed for two primary groups:

Students and graduates of U.S. medical schools accredited by the Liaison Committee on Medical Education (LCME) or the Commission on Osteopathic College Accreditation (COCA).

Students and graduates of international medical schools who wish to practice medicine in the United States. These individuals must obtain ECFMG Certification before entering an accredited U.S. residency program and before taking Step 3.

For international medical graduates (IMGs), the pathway involves additional steps. They must first create a MyIntealth account and apply for ECFMG Certification, which verifies their medical school credentials and completion of required examinations. ECFMG Certification requires passing Step 1 and Step 2 CK, along with verification of clinical and communication skills through one of six ECFMG Pathways.

Registration and the 2026 Service Transition

A significant operational change occurred in January 2026 with the USMLE Service Transition. All USMLE Step exam services—including exam registration, score report delivery, and customer service—are now consolidated under the USMLE co-sponsors, FSMB and NBME.

For international medical students and graduates, this means registering for all Step exams through the FSMB's USMLE Portal rather than through ECFMG as was previously required. ECFMG continues to determine eligibility for ECFMG Certification, but the registration process itself has been streamlined under the unified portal.

Examinees take their exams at Prometric test centers worldwide for Step 1 and Step 2 CK, and at Prometric centers located within the United States for Step 3.

Scoring and Performance Data

Step 1 is reported as pass/fail, while Step 2 CK and Step 3 are reported with numerical scores and performance profiles. For Step 2 CK, score reports include an estimated probability of passing, which provides examinees with a statistical measure of their readiness.

Pass rates vary by examinee group. In the most recent data, first-attempt pass rates for Step 1 are approximately 91% for U.S. MD students, 89% for U.S. DO students, and 72% for international medical graduates. Repeat takers have markedly lower pass rates, near 54%, which underscores the importance of thorough preparation before the first attempt.

For Step 2 CK, scores are reported on a scale with a passing standard of 218. Performance percentiles are also provided, allowing examinees to understand how their performance compares to that of other test takers.

Test Day Logistics and Security

USMLE Step 1 is a long and demanding exam administered in a single 8-hour testing session. It consists of 7 60-minute blocks of questions, with up to 40 items per block and a maximum of 280 total questions. In addition to question time, examinees receive at least 45 minutes of break time and an optional 15-minute tutorial at the beginning.

Test centers implement strict security procedures. Before entering the testing area—and every time returning from a break—examinees undergo fingerprint scanning, pocket emptying, glasses inspection, and metal detector scanning. These measures maintain the integrity of the examination and ensure no examinee has an unfair advantage.

Personal items must be stored in lockers, and the testing center provides essential materials including wired headphones, noise-cancelling earmuffs, and laminated scrap paper with a marker. Snacks and beverages can be stored in lockers for use during authorized breaks.

Retake Policies and Attempt Limits

The USMLE program imposes clear limits on retaking examinations. If an examinee passes a Step, they are generally not permitted to retake it, except to comply with time limits imposed by a medical licensing authority.

If an examinee fails a Step, they must reapply and pay the appropriate fees to retake the exam. The program limits the total number of times an examinee can take the same Step to four attempts, including incomplete attempts. Examinees who have attempted a Step four or more times without passing are ineligible to apply for any Step in the USMLE sequence.

There are also timing restrictions: examinees may not take the same examination more than three times within a 12-month period. A fourth attempt must be at least 12 months after the first attempt and at least six months after the most recent attempt.

For ECFMG Certification purposes, all examination requirements must be satisfied within a seven-year period. If this window is not met, the earliest passing performance may no longer be valid for certification.

The USMLE's Role in Medical Licensure

Passing all three Steps of the USMLE is required for medical licensure in the United States. State medical boards use USMLE results as a common evaluation system to assess whether applicants possess the knowledge and skills necessary to provide safe and effective patient care.

For IMGs, ECFMG Certification is a prerequisite for entering an accredited U.S. graduate medical education program and for taking Step 3. The certification process verifies that international medical graduates have met the same standards of medical knowledge and clinical skills as their U.S. counterparts.

The pathway to licensure typically involves completing medical school, passing Step 1 and Step 2 CK, obtaining ECFMG Certification for IMGs, matching into a residency program, passing Step 3, completing residency training, and applying for a state medical license.

Preparing for the USMLE

Effective preparation for the USMLE requires a strategic approach combining content review, question practice, and self-assessment. Most students use a combination of resources including question banks, comprehensive review books, and practice examinations.

The transition of Step 1 to pass/fail has shifted strategic emphasis. While Step 1 still requires thorough preparation, the pressure for a high numerical score has been removed. Step 2 CK now carries greater weight as a scored examination, particularly for residency applications.

Structured preparation options: For students seeking personalized guidance, tutoring services can provide structured study plans tailored to individual strengths and weaknesses. Dedicated Prep, a tutoring service offering 1-on-1 coaching from high-scoring tutors, emphasizes data-driven approaches to USMLE preparation, including analysis of NBME performance reports to identify weak areas and create targeted study plans. Another well-known option is Kaplan Medical, which offers comprehensive review courses and Qbank resources for each Step.

Frequently Asked Questions About the USMLE

What is the USMLE?

The USMLE (United States Medical Licensing Examination) is a three-step examination program sponsored by the FSMB and NBME. It assesses a physician's ability to apply medical knowledge and skills required for safe patient care, and is accepted by all 50 state medical boards for licensure.

What are the three steps of the USMLE?

Step 1 assesses basic sciences and is now pass/fail. Step 2 CK assesses clinical knowledge and is scored. Step 3 assesses independent clinical decision-making and is taken during residency.

What is a passing score on USMLE Step 1?

Step 1 is reported as pass/fail, with a passing standard of 196 on the three-digit scale, roughly equivalent to about 60% correct answers.

How many attempts are allowed on the USMLE?

Examinees may take the same Step up to four times, including incomplete attempts. You cannot take the same exam more than three times within 12 months.

Do IMGs need ECFMG Certification for the USMLE?

Yes. International medical graduates must obtain ECFMG Certification, which requires passing Step 1 and Step 2 CK, before entering a U.S. residency program and before taking Step 3.

Conclusion

The USMLE is more than an examination—it is the gateway to medical licensure in the United States. Its three-Step structure is designed to assess medical knowledge and clinical skills at progressive stages of training, from basic sciences through independent clinical decision-making.

For U.S. medical students and international medical graduates alike, understanding the structure, requirements, and policies of the USMLE is essential for navigating the pathway to residency and licensure. The 2026 Service Transition has streamlined registration processes, while the pass/fail change for Step 1 has shifted strategic focus to Step 2 CK and beyond.

Whether preparing for Step 1, planning for ECFMG Certification, or studying for Step 3 during residency, a thorough understanding of the USMLE empowers medical graduates to approach each examination with clarity and purpose.
